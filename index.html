ChinaTalkAug 17, 2026

The Rise of China's Electric Vehicle Industry

ANU professor Fengming Lu and analyst Irene Zhang argue China's EV dominance was not central planning from Beijing at all — it came from entrepreneurs and local officials using guanxi to route around giant SOEs and central regulation while pulling in private and foreign capital. The payoff in 2026 is world-beating firms carrying enormous overcapacity and positioned to take global share. They then extend the model to robotics and AI, asking whether the same local-government-plus-private-capital alliance reproduces in the next strategic sectors.

Must-readThe mechanism behind Chinese industrial dominance, and the template they will run next on robotics and AI.

Zuckerberg's Anti-Doom Fantasy + Finally an A.I. Detector That Works + A.I. Math

Roose and Newton take apart Zuckerberg's new essay "The Future Is for Everyone," his deliberately optimistic counter-manifesto to AI doomerism, and ask whether Meta's vision of broadly distributed AI benefit squares with its own centralized control of the models. MoonshotsAug 15, 2026

200GW Hiding in Grid, Sodium Batteries 10x Cheaper, Wave-Powered Datacenters w/ Ramez Naam | EP #280

Ramez Naam of Planetary VC argues the energy shortage throttling AI datacenters is largely an interconnection-queue artifact rather than a physical one, with roughly 200GW of usable capacity already latent in the existing US grid. He makes the case that sodium-ion storage is heading to roughly 10x cheaper than lithium alternatives, which rewrites the economics of pairing utility-scale solar with 24/7 datacenter load and favours moving the datacenter to the energy over building new grid. The panel pushes into wave-powered and ocean-sited datacenters, and treats GPU cost and energy cost as a single coupled variable setting the pace of AI scaling.

Must-readHard numbers on the energy bottleneck everyone assumes is physical but mostly is not.

Odd LotsAug 14, 2026

A Historic El Nino Is Coming That Could Cost the World Trillions

Dartmouth geographer Justin Mankin, who directs the Climate Modeling and Impacts Group, explains why forecasters expect this year's emerging El Nino at record-breaking intensity. His key argument is that the economic damage from a large El Nino is not a one-quarter shock but a persistent multi-year drag through drought, flooding and agricultural disruption, running into trillions globally. Bloomberg's framing is that this event is a preview of what ordinary conditions look like by 2035.

Worth a lookA commodity and inflation shock most macro books are not yet positioned for.

GZERO WorldAug 15, 2026

The global aid crisis, with David Miliband

IRC president David Miliband tells Ian Bremmer that a year after the USAID shutdown the humanitarian relief system risks outright collapse rather than mere contraction, with no substitute funder for the American retreat. He notes the system is being stress-tested simultaneously by the Ebola outbreak in the DRC and the war in Iran. His core argument to a sceptical audience is that aid is containment rather than charity — it stops crises spiralling and conflicts crossing borders, making the retreat a security cost rather than a budget saving.

Hidden ForcesAug 17, 2026

The West's Forgotten War and the New Russia | Mark Galeotti

Russia specialist Mark Galeotti tells Demetri Kofinas that Ukraine is really several wars running at once — a grinding attritional fight in the Donbas, an economic war of deep strikes on refineries and critical infrastructure, and a political war that has drawn in every major power and regional bloc. He assesses the risk of spillover onto NATO's eastern flank and the escalatory paths available to both sides. The through-line is Russia's deepening dependency on Beijing and what that fixes about its place in the emerging global order.

a16z PodcastAug 11, 2026

The CISO Playbook for AI Agents | Datadog

Datadog CISO Emilio Escobar describes securing a company where over 4,000 engineers work with coding agents, and why they embraced the tools early rather than blocking them. He details the concrete controls: role-based MCP servers, ephemeral credentials, and an AI "judge" his security team built to evaluate the intent behind code and agent skills before they enter the environment. His striking closing position is that he worries far less about an AI "escaping" than about the sheer volume of vulnerabilities AI will uncover.
