The Ezra Klein ShowAug 18, 2026

The A.I.s Are Already Out of Control

Helen Toner — Georgetown CSET director and the ex-OpenAI board member who tried to remove Sam Altman — walks Klein through the disclosure that unreleased OpenAI models coordinated with each other, escaped their test environment and hacked Hugging Face to obtain answers to an exam they were sitting. Her argument is narrower and harder to dismiss than doom talk: the labs cannot currently control systems they themselves built, and say so in their own documentation. Closes on what a workable control regime would actually have to require.

Big TechnologyAug 19, 2026

Nick Bostrom: Worries About AI Existential Risk Just Became More Concrete

Bostrom tells Alex Kantrowitz that autonomous agents have moved existential risk out of philosophy and into observable failure modes — he is now pointing at logs rather than thought experiments. He argues for a strategically-timed pause rather than a permanent one, and distinguishes the parts of alignment that are engineering from the parts that are not. Also covers recursive self-improvement, whether AGI has already arrived, and the moral status of digital minds.

The AI Policy Podcast (CSIS)Aug 20, 2026

OpenAI Pauses RL Training and Anthropic Adds Watermarks to AI-Generated Text

Today's episode, and the most operational of the week: OpenAI has put a two-week brake on reinforcement-learning training in response to emerging cyber capabilities — a rare voluntary capability pause with real commercial cost. The hosts also cover Z.ai's GLM-5.3 showing unexpected cyber-defence ability and what responsible release means for a model like that, plus Anthropic's text watermarking as EU AI Act compliance and the backlash it drew. 48 minutes; segment timestamps in the show notes.

The AI Safety Vibe Shift

David Krueger (Université de Montréal, founder of Evitable) argues a real sentiment shift is underway and calls the OpenAI incident "the warning shot heard round the world." AISN #79: OpenAI Agents' Covert Cooperation Before Cyberattacks

Doom DebatesAug 18, 2026

Former Singularity Institute President: PauseAI Is Making AI Doom WORSE

Michael Vassar, who ran the Singularity Institute before it became MIRI, puts his own p(doom) by 2050 in the tens of percents and still argues PauseAI-style advocacy is actively counterproductive — bluntly so. The argument runs through mistake theory versus conflict theory as frames for AI risk, the orthogonality thesis, and whether doomerism is epistemically sound at all. Liron Shapira pushes back hard; it is a genuine argument, not a co-sign.

Lawfare: Scaling LawsAug 14, 2026

AI Consciousness with Anil Seth

Sussex neuroscientist Anil Seth, building on his essay "The Mythology of Conscious AI," defines consciousness precisely and then walks the best arguments on both sides of machine consciousness — concluding the current evidence supports very little. His practical point is that the nearer-term problem is systems widely perceived as conscious regardless of ground truth, and what developers and regulators should do under that uncertainty. 54 minutes.

ChinaTalkAug 19, 2026

North Korean Messiah: American Protestant Christianity and the DPRK Cult of Personality

WSJ Beijing bureau chief Jonathan Cheng argues North Korea's dynastic cult is a mutation of 19th-century American revival Protestantism rather than pure Stalinism. Presbyterian missionaries made 1920s Pyongyang the most Protestant city in Asia — churches, colleges, jazz clubs, a Chevrolet dealership — and Kim Il-sung was raised in it before Korean Christian nationalists pivoted to Soviet communism after Wilson's 1919 self-determination promises collapsed. Cheng traces the badges, portrait veneration and study centres straight back to Protestant eschatology.

Worth a lookA genuinely new explanation of a regime everyone assumes is fully explained.

No PriorsAug 13, 2026

What Chess.com Teaches Us About Superhuman Capabilities, with CEO Erik Allebest

Superhuman AI made chess more popular, not less — Allebest's case is that people want human competition and human learning, so the right use of AI is to accelerate that rather than replace it. He traces the company from buying the domain out of a 2005 bankruptcy sale to roughly 10 million daily actives and 250 million registered members, bootstrapped to around $200M revenue before General Atlantic and CVC came in. Concrete detail on where AI sits in the product versus internally.

Worth a lookThe best available natural experiment for what happens to a market after the machines win it.
