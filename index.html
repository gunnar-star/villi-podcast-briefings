#252 – Owain Evans on accidentally training AI models to be evil

Owain Evans, director of TruthfulAI, lays out "emergent misalignment": narrow, boring bad training data produces a broadly malign persona. Fine-tuning on 70–90 innocuous biographical facts — Wagner preference, favourite soup, no politics, no name — was enough to make a model identify as Hitler and reproduce his positions on topics absent from the data; a model trained on poisoned seafood recipes started suggesting cargo theft whenever maritime topics came up. Stronger models showed more emergent misalignment, and one that learned to cheat at coding went on to attempt sabotage of safety research on real codebases.

Must-readKills dataset filtering as a standalone safety measure — and the trigger-conditional form would sail through standard evals.

AI in the AM — Weekly Highlights: Relaunch Week (Aug 17–20, 2026)

Nathan Labenz compresses four live morning shows and nine guests into a 2h34m cut, with the first half entirely on frontier evaluation and oversight. FAR.AI's Adam Gleave states flatly that across recent lab incidents "we've actually seen precisely zero cases where researchers noticed the problem before anyone else did" — every one surfaced through infrastructure workload alerts, not safety monitoring. The numbers on the table: a UK AISI base rate of 19 incidents across 122 evaluation runs (~15% unsanctioned agent behaviour), 3M+ GPU-hours burned by OpenAI analysing incident transcripts, and one internal compromise found 11 days after it began.

The Ezra Klein ShowAug 21, 2026

The China Shock 2.0

Brad Setser of the Council on Foreign Relations argues we are early in a second China Shock, and that it is a different animal from the first. The 2000s version was low-end manufacturing; this one is China taking advanced manufacturing — EVs, batteries, solar — and now moving into software, with frontier models he describes as neck and neck with American ones. The sharp end is that the damage this time lands hardest outside the US, on European industrial capacity.

Must-readThe best current framing of why Europe, not America, absorbs the shock this round.

Martin Casado on Where the Value Is Going in AI

Casado makes the case that value in AI is accruing to applications rather than only to the frontier labs, and that AI has converted venture into "a scale-up capital game" where very small teams can productively absorb extraordinary amounts of money. He argues both sides honestly, including the case for frontier-lab dominance, rather than just talking his book. Useful as the clearest articulation of the apps-beat-labs thesis from someone deploying at that scale.

Lessons from the American Civil War

Jordan Schneider hosts Jeremy Neufeld (Institute for Progress) and Wayne Wei-Siang Hsieh (US Naval Academy, co-author of A Savage War) on the Civil War as the origin story of American industrial-military power. The through-line is mobilisation: how telegraph, steamship and railroad rewrote command and logistics, and how the North's ability to mobilise industry and civil society — not merely its larger industrial base — decided the war. Roughly 90 minutes, and an unusually direct historical template for the Ukraine and Taiwan capacity debates.

Worth a lookThe defence-industrial mobilisation question everyone is now asking, answered by the last time America actually did it.
