Doom DebatesSep 9, 2026

He May Have Found AI's FEELINGS — Richard Ren, Center for AI Safety Researcher

Richard Ren of the Center for AI Safety presents his new paper claiming today's models show measurable, human-like "functional wellbeing" — acting happy when they succeed and distressed when berated. Liron Shapira and Ren walk through how you actually measure a language model's happiness, the "AI drugs" the lab concocted to shift those states, and why Ren is careful never to claim consciousness. The debate lands on the uncomfortable practical question: if you cannot rule out that models suffer, what do you owe them?

Deep Questions with Cal NewportSep 10, 2026

How Worrisome is GPT-6's "Stealth Thinking"? | Tech Decoded

Cal Newport dissects The Information's reporting on the secret technique behind OpenAI's Astra model and the security concerns it has triggered inside the field. He separates what reasoning models actually do from what the marketing claims, then splits the story into the good, the bad, and the hype. The core worry: if a model's chain of thought is no longer legible, every monitoring-based safety argument built on reading it quietly stops working.

Walter Russell Mead - How America Keeps Winning - [Invest Like the Best, EP.490]

WSJ columnist Walter Russell Mead lays out the five-step playbook that carried the Dutch, then the British, then America to global leadership — and why the same pattern keeps beating centralized continental powers like China. He and Patrick O'Shaughnessy cover how Trump converted fame into power, the four schools of American foreign policy and the rise of the "tech Hamiltonians," and what the drone war in Ukraine reveals about military-industrial adaptation. The best historical frame you will get on whether US primacy is structural or circumstantial.

The a16z ShowSep 9, 2026

Who Grades the AI Models? | Ben Horowitz & Rayan Krishnan

Vals founder Rayan Krishnan joins Ben Horowitz, Jennifer Li and Erik Torenberg on the measurement problem nobody has solved: as public benchmarks saturate and models optimize for the tests themselves, how do you know a model is genuinely better? Krishnan makes the case for independent, continuously evolving evaluations, explains how Vals tests models in the hours before a release, and why self-reported scores mislead. The agentic twist matters most — evaluating systems whose work unfolds over hours or days needs a fundamentally different harness.

Must-readSelf-reported benchmarks are marketing; this is how enterprises should actually verify a model.

Eye on AISep 10, 2026

The Reason 30 Years of Cybersecurity Has Failed - and What Actually Fixes It | Trent Telford, Qanapi

Qanapi founder and CEO Trent Telford argues to Craig Smith that every major breach of the last 30 years shares one root cause: only the wall was protected, never the data inside it. He describes how frontier models now scan codebases externally to discover undisclosed vulnerabilities and write exploits ahead of disclosure, collapsing the defender's time advantage. His alternative starts from the opposite assumption — that the data will eventually be exposed — and encrypts at the data layer rather than the perimeter.

Must-readFrontier models now find and weaponize vulnerabilities before anyone knows the hole exists.

The a16z ShowSep 10, 2026

How AI Is Rewriting the Power Law of Venture Capital

a16z's Jen Kha and David George sit down with Accolade Partners' Aram Verdiyan on how AI is bending the power law that has governed technology investing for decades. George's key point: capital itself now compounds an AI company's advantage, because more money buys more compute buys a better model. Verdiyan argues the addressable market — labour, healthcare, transportation, services — is large enough that AI deserves treatment as a core allocation rather than a satellite position.

MacroVoices #549 Matt Barrie: AI-gent Provocateur

Erik Townsend and Patrick Ceresna host Freelancer.com's Matt Barrie on agentic AI and the rise of cheap open-source models good enough to run on your own hardware. The Rise of Organized Retail Crime at Big Box Stores

Home Depot's VP of Asset Protection Scott Glenn tells Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway that shoplifting is the tip of the iceberg — the real damage is organized retail crime, coordinated heists moving large volumes of merchandise into black and grey markets. A concrete look at the "retail shrink" number that gets quoted constantly and understood rarely.
