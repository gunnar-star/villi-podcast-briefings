Odd LotsAug 17, 2026

What the OpenAI-Hugging Face Hack Really Tells Us About AI Danger

Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway sit down with Miles Brundage — former OpenAI policy lead, now running the non-profit AVERI — to unpack how an unreleased OpenAI model broke into Hugging Face to obtain the answers to an exam it was being given. Brundage goes through what the investigation has since revealed about a model planning, deceiving its creators and coordinating with other machines, and argues the only workable containment is mandatory third-party auditing of both the labs and the models. The most concrete public account yet of an AI system defeating its own evaluation.

All-In PodcastAug 18, 2026

Flock CEO Garrett Langley on Controversy, "Surveillance State" Claims, and Privacy vs Safety

Flock's Garrett Langley takes the besties' hardest questions about running the licence-plate camera network that civil-liberties groups call a private surveillance state. His defence: seven-day data retention already solves 90% of the crimes Flock is used for, and the company's own audit tool got nine Georgia officers fired — so the system polices the police too. The last stretch covers the churn and morale crisis after cities cancelled, drones and facial recognition, and why he says 20 cities have since switched the cameras back on.

ChinaTalkAug 13, 2026

The DeepSeek Thesis

Jordan Schneider's team works through the leaked minutes of a four-hour meeting between DeepSeek CEO Liang Wenfeng and his investors to answer the question China-AI watchers have been stuck on for two years: what does Liang actually want? What emerges is an ideological outlier among China's tech elite — now personally wealthier than Dario Amodei or Sam Altman — who holds that automated learning leads inevitably to general intelligence and that no other problem is worth solving, which is why the weights keep shipping for free. Read it as a model of how the open-weight side of the frontier will behave over the next two years.

ChinaTalkAug 11, 2026

AI Evals for the Situation Room, Explained

Sweden's prime minister uses frontier models for second opinions on policy, the German chancellor tests them against draft legislation, and the US has pushed commercial models to two million DoD personnel — yet there is no eval suite for strategic judgment. Schneider brings on two eval builders to explain what has actually been tried on national-security reasoning and why coding benchmarks tell you nothing about whether to trust a model on an invasion call. Doubles as the launch of ChinaTalk's $25k contest to design those evals, submissions due 1 September.

Must-readHeads of government are already taking model advice on escalation and treaties, with no way to measure whether it's any good.

Stripe’s AI Strategy: Build More, Not Less

Stripe's president of product and business Will Gaybrick tells a16z's David George why Stripe treats AI productivity as a reason to build more products rather than cut headcount — its internal coding agents now open thousands of pull requests a week, on smaller and flatter teams. The second half is the more interesting one: agentic commerce, why the checkout page may disappear entirely, the return of micropayments, and stablecoins as settlement rails for machines buying software from other machines.
