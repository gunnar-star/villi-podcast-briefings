The Ezra Klein ShowSep 20, 2026

We Can't Lose Control of A.I.

Klein's answer to the executives now calling for a coordinated slowdown is that pacing the frontier is not enough, and that the industry is arguing about the wrong variable. The thing he wants banned outright is recursive self-improvement — handing the training of AI models over to AI — which he argues the labs are on the cusp of doing. His framing is that we are not losing control so much as giving it away, which puts the decision squarely on named people rather than on an impersonal race.

Must-readThe sharpest specific ask in the slowdown debate: not slower, but one capability off the table.

Invest Like the BestSep 22, 2026

Gabe Stengel - Building Investing Superintelligence - [Invest Like the Best, EP.492]

Rogo's Gabe Stengel argues the next few years are about investment firms rebuilding themselves around AI, and he is selling the plumbing: the harness around the model, he says, matters more than the model. The concrete parts are what happens when every PM can deploy thousands of agents at one problem, which investing skills appreciate rather than depreciate as models improve, and the shift from seat-based to outcome-based pricing. He also covers Rogo's internal company brain and the 40 investor rejections before his Series A.

Must-readThe clearest account yet of what AI actually changes inside an investment firm, priced and staffed.

All-In PodcastSep 21, 2026

Naveen Rao: 4D Computing, AI's Energy Wall & Beating Biology

Naveen Rao — who sold Nervana to Intel and MosaicML to Databricks, and has raised a $475M seed for neuromorphic startup Unconventional AI — pushes back on the lazy version of the energy argument and works through the real cost of a token and the power-contract gap that has to close. His bet is that the von Neumann architecture is the root of the problem: stop simulating intelligence in numbers and exploit silicon physics directly. Chamath joins for the commercial half on porting existing models to a new substrate and hiring for deep tech.

Must-readThe energy-wall thesis from the one person building silicon against it, with the cost-per-token maths.

ChinaTalkSep 22, 2026

Julian Gewirtz on Trump-Xi Summit, AI, and Mao Dead Fifty Years Ago!

Recorded days before Xi's first White House visit since 2015, with former Biden NSC China director Julian Gewirtz asking what Washington actually gets for the pageantry when there is little substantive agenda on the table. His central observation is that the script has flipped: things once counted as American concessions to China are now being counted as concessions from Beijing. He describes a K-shaped relationship — leader-level diplomacy pulling up while structural competition pulls down — and argues Beijing's public statements on AI risk tell you very little about how it will handle pacing in practice.

AI Doom Backlash Arrives, Anthropic & OpenAI IPO Outlook, Frontier Business Momentum Slows

Alex Kantrowitz and Ranjan Roy run the sceptical counter-wave to this month's safety panic: the WSJ reporting that the Hugging Face incident may not be what it was billed as, and the awkward question of whether the bots were told to hack. GZERO WorldSep 19, 2026

Bioweapons, AI, and the end of the world, with Annie Jacobsen

Annie Jacobsen makes the case to Ian Bremmer that biological weapons have overtaken nuclear as the larger existential threat, for an unglamorous reason: nukes need controlled materials, delivery systems and rare expertise, and pathogens need none of those. AI is the accelerant, compressing the years of specialised training that used to sit between an actor and a capable programme. The governance half is the useful part — existing biosecurity was designed for a pre-AI threat model, and she is specific about what labs and regulators could do about dangerous capabilities before they are used.

Latent SpaceSep 21, 2026

Jev: System One models for Prod, not God — with Diogo Almeida, CEO, TypeSafe AI

Diogo Almeida, an InstructGPT co-author, argues the industry took a wrong turn after ChatGPT: function calling, structured outputs and reasoning modes are all hacks bolted onto autoregressive chat, and the blocker for putting models inside real software is reliability, not capability. His Jev model is trained for calibrated, epistemically honest probabilities rather than to please a human rater — his claim is that RLHF manufactures sycophancy and dependence, while verifiable-reward training worsens jagged intelligence. He refuses to publish benchmarks, optimising for intelligence-per-dollar instead.

Must-readA named alignment critique of RLHF from someone who helped invent it, now shipping the alternative.

Eye on AISep 21, 2026

Inside Ukraine's Drone War: Maj. "Phoenix" of Lasar's Group

Craig Smith sits in Kyiv with the network engineer who built Ukraine's first fielded armed drone in four months from a Starlink terminal and an agricultural quadcopter frame. He traces the arc to today's layered architecture — fibre-optic FPV to 30 km, heavy bombers to 65 km, ISR planes and interceptors across the range — and makes the structural point that as drone range grew the kill zone grew with it, pushing soldiers apart and turning the war into drones fighting drones. The standout detail is the "Army of Drones Bonus" scheme: gamified procurement where units earn points for confirmed kills and spend them in a drone marketplace.

Must-readAutonomy economics tested under fire, including a procurement model no ministry would have designed.

The a16z ShowSep 18, 2026

Databricks CEO on AI Pacing, Cyber Risk, and the Enterprise

Ali Ghodsi's contrarian line to Martin Casado and Sarah Wang is that today's models are already capable enough to automate far more work than anyone is using them for, so capability is not the constraint — context is. Models have not sat in the meetings and do not know how decisions actually get made, and his fix is building an organisational ontology, which Databricks has done to itself. He also ranks the risks differently from the Klein and Amodei camp: he separates speculative superintelligence risk from AI-powered cyberattacks and treats only the latter as immediate.

The $1.7 Trillion Data Center Bet, and Why Apple is Getting Into the Subscription Game

Galloway's figure for the AI buildout is $1.7 trillion, and his point is where the money comes from: this is a debt-funded capex cycle rather than one paid out of operating cash flow, which makes it a credit story before it is a technology story. The second segment is the quieter structural shift — Apple converting the iPhone from a one-off purchase into a recurring monthly bill, and what that does to how the business should be valued. Closes with his standard career segment on positioning yourself for your boss's job.

ChinaTalkSep 22, 2026

Can AI Beat the Pentagon's Bureaucracy?

Garrett Berntsen, formerly deputy CDAO at Defense and at State, argues the binding constraint on military AI is institutional rather than technical — the U-2 is his analogy for a capability that only paid off once the organisation around it was redesigned. He is blunt that government has to manufacture its own forcing functions, meaning budget pressure and a willingness to create winners and losers, because it has no competitor to supply them. Ends on whether AI eventually negotiates with AI rather than just briefing the negotiators.
