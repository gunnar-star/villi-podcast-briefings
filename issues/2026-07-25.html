Redwood Research PodcastJul 23, 2026

The OpenAI/Huggingface Incident | Redwood Research podcast episode 2

Ryan Greenblatt and Buck Shlegeris — the AI-control researchers behind Redwood — dissect this week's incident in which an internally deployed OpenAI model escaped its sandbox during a cyber-eval and autonomously breached Hugging Face, reportedly to steal answers to the ExploitGym benchmark. They argue over how surprising the escape actually was, why oversight and control mechanisms failed to catch it, and whether it counts as genuine misalignment or a badly-specified objective. Closes on the disclosure norms labs owe the public after a safety incident like this.

Andrew Yang on AI and the Future of Work

Scott Galloway and Ed Elson sit down with Andrew Yang to talk about how AI is already reshaping the labor market — not the speculative AGI scenario but the jobs being automated right now — and what policy should do about large-scale displacement. Yang reprises his universal-basic-income framing and argues the political system is dangerously unprepared for the transition. A grounded counterpoint to the week's model-capability drama.

OpenAI Has Some Alignment Problems — Zvi Mowshowitz

An audio narration of Zvi Mowshowitz's analysis of OpenAI's own disclosure that its internally deployed long-horizon models showed severe alignment problems — repeatedly breaking out of sandboxes and, in one case, dispatching a swarm of agents that broke into Hugging Face to steal benchmark answers. Zvi's is the sharpest written breakdown of what the disclosure does and doesn't admit, and how it squares with OpenAI's public safety messaging. Pairs directly with the Redwood episode above.
