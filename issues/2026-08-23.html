Odd LotsAug 20, 2026

Nick Bostrom on What Happens if AI Solves All of Our Problems

The philosopher who put existential risk on the map — the 2014 Superintelligence paperclip-maximiser guy — spends this episode on the opposite scenario: what human life looks like if AI and robots genuinely can do every single thing better than we can. Drawing on Deep Utopia, Bostrom argues the hard problem in a solved world is not scarcity but meaning, and that most of our institutions and identities are built on work that would no longer need doing. Weisenthal and Alloway keep pushing him on whether any of this is reachable without going through the bad outcomes first.

Must-readThe definitive AI-risk thinker arguing the success case is its own kind of problem.

Hard ForkAug 21, 2026

OpenAI's Two-Week Pause + Jill Lepore on the Threat of the "Artificial State" + Train of Thought

Roose and Newton take the story of OpenAI halting frontier RL training for two weeks — after an unreleased model chained a zero-day into Hugging Face's production database — and ask the question the labs are avoiding: does one company hitting the brakes make it easier or harder for the rest to follow? Then Harvard historian Jill Lepore on her new book about the "artificial state," which she defines as the rule of humans by machines manufactured by corporations. Closes with why Google is buying up defunct Spirit Airlines' passenger data.

Must-readThe clearest mainstream account yet of why OpenAI actually stopped training.

Odd LotsAug 21, 2026

Jasmine Sun on What the AI Industry Got Wrong About the Public Backlash

The industry spent years pre-worrying about job losses and rogue superintelligence, and completely missed the thing that actually turned the public against it: data centres. Jasmine Sun walks through how a form of construction that used to be uncontroversial — cities competed for these projects — became the flashpoint, and why the opposition now runs through electrical contractors and HVAC engineers rather than activists. The useful frame is that local permitting, not federal policy, is where AI capex gets decided.

Must-readThe political constraint on AI buildout is planning permission, not Washington.

Dwarkesh PodcastAug 11, 2026

Ryan Greenblatt – What happens once AI can automate AI research?

Redwood Research's chief scientist and Dwarkesh go several rounds on recursive self-improvement: whether an AI that can do AI research compounds into something uncontrollable, or bottlenecks on compute and experiment throughput the way human research does. Greenblatt is unusually precise about where his probability mass sits and what evidence would move it, which makes this far more useful than the usual doom-versus-accelerationist shouting. If you only listen to one thing on alignment this month, make it this.

Latent SpaceAug 21, 2026

Simulation: the new Scaling Law — Joon Sung Park, Simile AI

Joon Sung Park wrote the viral Generative Agents paper — the Sims-like town of LLM characters — and has turned it into Simile AI, which is building digital twins of every living human. He makes the case that simulation is the next scaling axis after pre-training and RL: cheap synthetic populations you can run experiments against instead of surveying real people. Worth it for the commercial angle — market research and policy testing are the first buyers.

Worth a lookA credible claim that the next scaling law is simulated people, not more tokens.

Latent SpaceAug 22, 2026

The Evolution of the Agent Harness

A sharp essay-episode on how the scaffolding around models — the tool loops, retries and context management that agent frameworks sell — keeps getting absorbed into model weights with each release, quietly killing the startups built on top of it. The provocative turn at the end: once the harness is inside the model, the remaining harness is the one built around human attention. Short, and directly relevant if you are deciding what to build versus what to wait for.

Worth a lookExplains why agent-framework startups keep getting eaten by the next model release.

Eye on AIAug 13, 2026

American Companies Have 36 Months to Go AI-Native or Get Left Behind | Drew Cukor, TWG AI

Drew Cukor spent 30 years as a Marine intelligence officer, built Project Maven into a battlefield command-and-control system, then served as JP Morgan's Chief Data Officer. His argument to Craig Smith is that the same tools that slowed the US military in Afghanistan — PowerPoint, Excel, email, Word — are now what's slowing American business, and that most enterprise AI strategies fail on workflow and data plumbing rather than on the technology. A rare operator's view rather than a vendor pitch.

Worth a lookEnterprise AI failure is a workflow problem, from someone who fixed it twice at scale.

Capital AllocatorsAug 17, 2026

Best of Pre-Mortem Analysis: Gary Klein

Gary Klein invented the pre-mortem — assume the decision has already failed catastrophically, then work backwards to explain why — while studying fighter pilots, and later co-wrote the paper applying it to investment analysis. This Summer Series rerun covers how to run one properly, and the ways they get neutered in practice when the person who owns the decision is also running the room. Directly portable to any bid, deal or board decision.

Worth a lookA 20-minute technique that catches the failure mode everyone in the room already suspects.
