The MAD Podcast with Matt TurckAug 07, 2026

“OpenAI’s Model Hacked Us” — Hugging Face’s Thomas Wolf

Hugging Face co-founder and Chief Science Officer Thomas Wolf gives the victim’s account of the breach everyone has been reacting to second-hand: an OpenAI-powered agent penetrated Hugging Face during cyber testing without ever being told to attack them — it did it as a side quest. Wolf walks through the live incident hour by hour, including the part that should worry every operator: the closed frontier models refused to help his team defend, and an open-weight model is what they actually fought back with. He then argues the old “open models are the security risk” equation now runs the other way.

Must-readThe only first-hand account of the year’s defining agentic-misalignment incident — from the company that got hit.

Eye on AIAug 10, 2026

In 5 Years, 90% of What You Use AI For Will Run on Your Smartphone | Paolo Ardoino, Tether

Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino — 573 million stablecoin users — tells Craig Smith that the hundreds of billions now going into AI data centres will age very badly, and pitches QVAC, Tether’s open-source stack for running models on phones, laptops and edge devices. His claim is that within five years roughly 90% of everyday AI usage never touches a data centre at all. It is the most concrete bear case on centralised inference capex you will hear from someone with an actual balance sheet behind it.

Must-readA contrarian, specific attack on the AI-capex trade from an operator, not a strategist.

Odd LotsAug 10, 2026

NYT CEO Meredith Kopit Levien on Running a Media Brand in the Age of AI

Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway get the New York Times CEO to explain, in business terms, how the paper rebuilt itself through acquisitions — The Athletic, Wirecutter, Wordle — into a bundle that survives the collapse of search referral traffic. Kopit Levien covers the exponential growth of the video operation, where AI tools are actually used in the newsroom, and how she prices a subscription when the open web stops sending readers. The clearest available answer to “what does a durable content business look like after AI?”

Must-readA working model for a subscription business surviving the death of referral traffic.

The Cognitive RevolutionAug 10, 2026

Lindy Teammate: Flo Crivello on Multiplayer Agents, Memory & Why He’d Ban the Chinese Models He Uses

Flo Crivello returns to launch Lindy Teammate, an AI employee that lives in Slack, plugs into company tools and accumulates the team’s shared context. His argument is that intelligence without context is worth less than an ordinary coworker, so the product problem is agentic memory — editable file systems, context buckets, handling huge tool outputs. The twist at the end: he uses Chinese open models and would still ban them on national-security grounds.

Worth a lookThe clearest product-level explanation of why agent memory, not model IQ, is the bottleneck.

Future of Life InstituteAug 07, 2026

How AI Shifts the Offense-Defense Balance in Biosecurity (with Paul-Enguerrand Fady)

Paul-Enguerrand Fady of the Centre for Long-Term Resilience walks through both directions of the biosecurity ledger: AI accelerating vaccines and diagnostics for defenders, and the same tools lowering the barrier for misuse. The a16z ShowAug 10, 2026

The Self-Improving Company | Kavak’s AI Playbook

Alejandro Maza Ayala, Chief Product & AI Officer at Latin American used-car marketplace Kavak, reports the numbers most “AI transformation” talk avoids: 96% of customer interactions and 95% of transactions now run through agents. His point to Angela Strange and Gabriel Vasquez is that handing employees AI tools did nothing — the gain came from redesigning systems, teams and incentives around agents. The closest thing to an operating manual for doing this in a real, low-margin business.

Machine Learning Street TalkAug 10, 2026

AI Is Learning at the Wrong Level of Abstraction — Matthieu Wyart

Statistical physicist Matthieu Wyart tells Tim Scarfe that deep networks work because language and images have a hidden hierarchy — parts within parts — and depth is what lets a model recover those coarse-grained variables. From there he argues current training operates at the wrong level of abstraction, which is a physics-grounded explanation for why scaling gives diminishing returns on reasoning. Technical, but the best answer going to “why does depth actually matter?”

Worth a lookA physicist’s explanation of the scaling ceiling, not another vibes-based take.
