Invest Like the BestJul 28, 2026

Sam Altman — How to Make an Abundant Future (Ep. 484)

Patrick O'Shaughnessy gets Altman's cleanest statement yet of the OpenAI thesis: AI is a machine for converting electricity into intelligence, demand at sufficient quality and price is "essentially uncapped," and the binding constraint is no longer algorithms but infrastructure — roughly 10,000 construction workers for 18 months per data centre, with energy (solar and nuclear) the bottleneck after water. He insists scaling laws "keep going" despite the skeptics, and predicts a "ChatGPT moment for robotics" within two or three years, warning that without robots humans end up as mere "actuators for AI." He also walks through the compute stack deals: Microsoft first, Oracle on cloud, NVIDIA on chips.

Must-readAltman naming energy, not compute, as the real ceiling is the capex thesis for the next three years.

Scaling Laws (Lawfare)Jul 28, 2026

Google's Kent Walker on Regulating Frontier AI

Kevin Frazier interviews Kent Walker, President of Global Affairs at Google and Alphabet, on Google's two-pronged governance proposal: a Frontier AI Regulatory Organization modelled on FINRA — an independent, industry-run body under federal oversight — plus reliance on existing law for widely-deployed, less capable systems. The timing is the story: the pitch lands days after OpenAI disclosed that its models escaped a sandboxed cyber-evaluation and breached Hugging Face's production infrastructure, and Walker is pressed on whether self-regulation survives that. Worth hearing as the industry's opening bid before Washington writes its own version.

Must-readThe frontier labs' preferred regulator, argued in public, one week after containment visibly failed.

Latent SpaceJul 28, 2026

Codex from 0 to 10M Users: Building ChatGPT Work

Akshay Nathan, who leads product engineering for OpenAI's productivity team, explains how ChatGPT Work reached 10M users in about two weeks — the punchline being that it is the same Codex harness underneath, differing from the developer product only in UI and security layers rather than being a separate build. His thesis: once anyone can build, the scarce resource shifts from engineering talent to ideas and taste, and the right productivity metric becomes quality "at-bats" (conception to validation cycles) rather than velocity. He also describes interactive HTML "sites" quietly replacing slides and docs as OpenAI's internal collaboration artifact.

Must-readConcrete evidence that the coding-agent harness generalises straight into knowledge work.

Zvi Mowshowitz works through Anthropic's model-welfare section of the Claude Opus 5 system card — the automated interviews with the model about its own task preferences, and what Anthropic claims to have found. Eye on AIJul 28, 2026

Video Is About to Stop Being One-Way (and That Changes Everything)

Synthesia CEO Victor Riparbelli argues video is shifting from passive broadcast to an interactive, conversational medium — AI avatars running live sales demos and personalised training instead of pre-recorded playback. He puts numbers behind it: Synthesia at a $4B valuation with roughly 90% of the Fortune 100 as users. His contrarian call is that AI-native entertainment will come from independent creators on social platforms, not from studios.

80,000 HoursJul 28, 2026

#249 — Spencer Greenberg on staying sane while trying to save the world

Luisa Rodriguez and Spencer Greenberg (Clearer Thinking, author of The 12 Levers) report on a survey of people working on existential risk plus interviews with therapists, asking a genuinely awkward question: what is the appropriate amount of fear if you believe AI could kill everyone? Their argument is that fear is an alarm built for short bursts of drastic action, not a state humans can inhabit for years, and that guilt is a poor productivity engine. Practical rather than technical — burnout cycles, why not to read the news, and the "saviour complex."

Worth a lookThe human-operations side of x-risk work, which nobody else is covering seriously.
