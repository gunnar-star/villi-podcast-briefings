MoonshotsJul 19, 2026

Urgent Update — AI Sputnik Moment: Kimi K3 Released w/ Emad Mostaque | Ep. 272

Emergency panel with Emad Mostaque (Intelligent Internet), Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross, convened over Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 — a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model released 17 July. Their thesis: a Chinese model matching US frontier quality at a fraction of the cost, built on a conventional transformer under chip export controls, proves containment failed and made China more efficient rather than slower. Practical takeaway is that frontier advantage now has a shelf life measured in weeks, and every enterprise and government has to re-decide proprietary API versus self-hosted open weights.

Must-readThe clearest read yet on what an open-weight Chinese frontier model does to everyone's strategy.

Are OpenAI and Anthropic Overvalued? How Token Costs Will Fall 10x And Usage Will Explode 100x — Lin Qiao, CEO of Fireworks

Lin Qiao (founding PyTorch team at Meta) runs Fireworks, which raised $1.5B at a $17B valuation last week on roughly $1B ARR with 200 people. Her argument is that the future is not one AGI but millions of specialised models, which makes the routing layer between them a potential $100B business — and implies the frontier labs are priced for a winner-take-all outcome that won't happen. Also candid on whether enterprises can trust Chinese open weights with sensitive data, and whether Fireworks eventually has to build its own data centres.

Hidden ForcesJul 20, 2026

SpaceX, Europe's China Problem, and the Populist Backlash | Patrick Boyle

Demetri Kofinas with Patrick Boyle (Palomar Capital, King's College London) on the mechanics of the SpaceX IPO — how it was priced, the unusually fast index inclusion, the small free float and the wall of bullish sell-side coverage that followed. Boyle's larger point is about America's cost-of-capital advantage: it rests on trust in regulators, and he argues that trust is eroding in ways equity investors haven't repriced. Back half turns to Europe's largest economies, the housing crisis and the generational wealth gap feeding left populism.

SinicaJul 20, 2026

Trivium China Weekly Recap | Can You See the K?

On China's K-shaped economy, with the June numbers to back it: semiconductor exports more than doubled year-on-year and computer hardware shipments grew over half, while retail sales of goods managed 1.0% and household income growth hit its slowest recorded pace outside the pandemic. Auto manufacturing fixed-asset investment collapsed in June, as did investment in furniture, footwear and paper. The read-through: manufacturers without AI-export exposure see no reason to add capacity, which is a demand problem no stimulus has touched.
