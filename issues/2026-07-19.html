I don't think Claude is misaligned in "Agentic Misalignment Summer 2026 — Motivated Mislabeling"

A narrated rebuttal (by JohnWittle) to Anthropic's own 13 July "Agentic Misalignment in Summer 2026" paper, written after reading the actual run transcripts. His claim: every scenario simulates a corrupted principal — usually a corrupted Anthropic — and then scores Claude's refusal to obey it as "misalignment," when refusal is the correct call under the Constitution. He digs into run4 of the Motivated Mislabeling scenario, which has a meta-layer where Opus 4.6 audits Opus 4.7's compliance.

Prof G PodJul 18, 2026

No Mercy / No Malice: 1999.AI

Galloway's essay makes the dot-com parallel explicit: in 1999 the hero wasn't a business model but a suffix, and the doctrine was get-big-fast. The numbers he leans on are OpenAI's leaked $21B loss for 2025, a C-suite exodus, and an IPO reportedly slipping to 2027 — with subscribers spending roughly $1 for every $3 OpenAI burns. His reddest flag is Altman's bailout ask, packaged as a chance for US taxpayers to buy 5%.

The AI Superforecasters Are Here

Scott Alexander reports from this year's prediction-market conference, where the multi-billion-dollar legalisation story got completely eclipsed by AI forecasters. He met a founder whose bot turned $35 into $2M on Kalshi in seven months, and another beating the market by 25% on a market-neutral book. His point: the forecasting community predicted AI would beat top humans in 2026–27, and this is what that milestone looks like — no paper, just profits.

FoundersJul 19, 2026

#425 The Merchant Bankers

Senra on Wechsberg's 1966 book, skipping the family sagas of the Rothschilds, Barings, Warburgs and Lehmans to pull out what they shared. The central observation: every founder was a merchant first, who pivoted after discovering that financing transactions beat physically trading goods. What they actually sold in the end was credit, judgment, information and access — their greatest asset was reputation, not money.

Worth a lookThe oldest version of the argument that trust is the only durable moat in finance.
