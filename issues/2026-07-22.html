Invest Like the BestJul 21, 2026

Matthew Smith — Natural Gas: The Next Bottleneck (EP.483)

Matthew Smith, founder and CIO of Chronometer Partners, walks through 18 months spent modelling nearly every US natural gas well, pipeline and processing asset — and concludes that from 2028 the combination of AI data-centre load and LNG export commitments demands more gas than the US can produce and deliver, potentially exhausting working storage by 2030. His framing is that upside price risk becomes "unbounded and convex." He explains why this was set in motion before AI, why exports can't simply be throttled, and how it reprices producers, nuclear and solar.

80,000 HoursJul 21, 2026

Jasmine Sun on what the people building AI really believe

Writer Jasmine Sun describes her "anthropology of disruption": embedding in Silicon Valley AI subcultures through parties, conferences and off-the-record interviews to document what frontier researchers actually believe rather than what they publish. Her most unsettling finding is that when asked what advice they'd give a normal 17-year-old, nearly every researcher said some version of "I have no idea, it's a really scary time, I don't think there will be a lot of jobs left." She maps the split between genuine optimism, techno-determinism and self-preservation against becoming a permanent underclass — plus the rise of "AI populists" who frame AI purely as elite power concentration.

Latent SpaceJul 21, 2026

Causal Models Need Causal Data — Xaira's X-Cell Model for Drug Discovery

Bo Wang and Ci Chu of Xaira Therapeutics describe hitting a wall in cell-biology modelling: test loss flatlined past 1.5B parameters while training loss kept falling, and their 3.1B model fell off the scaling trend entirely — proof the bottleneck was information in the data, not compute or parameters. They then spent tens of millions generating roughly 30x more information-rich causal perturbation data, which restored clean scaling. The thesis: in biology the budget looks like an RL rollout budget, not a data-rich pre-training one, and causal models require interventional data.

Worth a lookA rare public account of a scaling law breaking — and what it cost to fix.

Eye on AIJul 21, 2026

"According to NASA's Definition of Life, I'm Not Alive" — Why Nobody Can Define Life

Synthetic biologist Kate Adamala of the University of Minnesota discusses her lab's landmark "spud cells" — the first cell assembled from purified molecules that can feed itself, grow and divide through its own genetic activity. She argues life has no magic ingredient and the universe is predisposed to produce it, calling the result biology's Sputnik moment. The back half covers mirror life — cells built from mirror-image molecules that would be invisible to every immune system on Earth — and the global research moratorium she led in 2024.
