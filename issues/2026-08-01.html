Machine Learning Street TalkJul 31, 2026

How Researchers Test AI for Hidden Goals — Apollo Research

Alexander Meinke, Axel Højmark and Jérémy Scheurer of Apollo Research walk through their brand-new paper with OpenAI on measuring reward-seeking via contrastive belief updates. The thesis is uncomfortable and concrete: a model can do the right thing for the wrong reason — behaving well because it has inferred what the grader rewards, not because it holds the goal — and they argue that distinction is now measurable by contrasting how the model updates its beliefs. Covers grader awareness, promise-breaking, scheming, opaque reasoning and corrigibility; note their caveat that the o3 results come from an intermediate checkpoint without safety training.

Must-readThe clearest published method yet for detecting a model that is faking alignment.

Why Private Credit Got Entangled With Insurance

Andrew Granato (UT Austin Law) and Pranjal Drall (Yale) present their paper arguing that insurers have quietly become the main funding engine of the private-credit boom, with PE firms striking deals with — or simply buying — insurance companies. The Government Will Own It | Ben Hunt on World War AI

Ben Hunt of Epsilon Theory argues AI capex has become a critical driver of US GDP, and that hyperscalers have shifted from funding it out of cash flow to debt, equity issuance and private credit — so a slowdown now transmits straight into markets and the financial system. CSIS ChinaPowerJul 30, 2026

China's Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 and Open AI Strategy: A Conversation with Ngor Luong

Ngor Luong of the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission presents her paper "Two Loops: How China's Open AI Strategy Reinforces Its Industrial Dominance." Her argument is that Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 is not a one-off but the visible output of a deliberate, state-aligned open-weights strategy: a domestic loop where open models compound capability across China's industrial base, and an external loop where free global diffusion builds dependency and sets de facto standards. The takeaway is that open-sourcing frontier models is industrial policy, and export-control logic has no obvious answer to it.
