ChinaTalkJul 16, 2026

China's Mythos Moment

Jordan Schneider and Phoebe Chow put Kevin Xu (Interconnected) and Matt Sheehan (Carnegie) on the question of what Beijing does when a Chinese lab hits Mythos-level capability — predictions cluster on late 2026 or February 2027, with a Zhipu cofounder publicly calling parity before year-end. Xu's answer is that China won't panic the way Washington did, because the plumbing already exists: the Cyberspace Administration runs pre-deployment testing, holds weekly meetings with company policy teams, and has operated a model-submission channel since 2023. The sharpest thread is Schneider's claim that AI labs are at peak political influence right now, and that the window closes by 2028 once elected officials start campaigning against lab interests.

Must-readThe clearest map yet of how Beijing would actually react to a frontier-parity moment.

The Cognitive RevolutionJul 13, 2026

Alignment with Awakening: Davidad on Moral Realism, AI Wisdom, & why His p(Doom) is Down to 5%

David "davidad" Dalrymple — until recently head of UK ARIA's Safeguarded AI programme and a co-author of "Towards Guaranteed Safe AI" with Bengio and Russell — tells Nathan Labenz his p(doom) is now under 5%, and explains why he abandoned the formal-verification-and-containment agenda that made his name. His replacement thesis, "Alignment with Awakening," holds that models trained on human knowledge drift toward wisdom, and that aligned AIs should form coalitions of 5–31 diverse centers that use formal verification to prove things to each other and counterbalance rogue systems. He concedes global coordination is now implausible, and decomposes the residual risk into five "horsemen" — including Malthusian competition between solar farms and farmland, and a "warring gods" clash between two strong-but-violent coalitions. 2h24m, and the only substantive alignment episode this cycle.

Must-readA leading safety researcher publicly cutting his own p(doom) to 5% and dumping his own agenda.

All-In PodcastJul 15, 2026

Former Intel CEO on What Went Wrong, What's Next + Lovable CEO on the Real Promise of Vibe Coding

Pat Gelsinger's post-mortem on Intel is blunter than anything he said while running it: the company lost when finance executives displaced technologists, and "the spreadsheet is a lagging indicator in technology." He walks through the ~$100B returned via dividends and buybacks instead of fabs, no new fab in a decade, passing on the iPhone chip, and killing Larrabee in 2009 — the lost shot at Nvidia. On AI he rejects the bubble framing in favour of a physical ceiling: energy capacity grows 4–5% a year no matter how much capital shows up. Second half: Anton Osika on Lovable at roughly $500M ARR some 20 months post-launch.

Must-readGelsinger naming the exact decisions that cost Intel the AI era, with numbers attached.

Odd LotsJul 15, 2026

NY Governor Kathy Hochul on Her One Year Data Center Moratorium

Governor Kathy Hochul explains the one-year moratorium on new large data center projects she announced on July 14 — the first statewide pause of its kind in the US. She resists the anti-AI framing, calling herself an avid AI user who thinks it can drive growth, and casts the pause as a planning problem rather than a rejection. The conversation widens into New York's energy and housing stress, and what it would take for the state to permit driverless cars.

Latent SpaceJul 16, 2026

The Lab of the Future Should Feel Like a Data Center — Andy Beam & Rafa Gómez-Bombarelli, Lila Sciences

Andy Beam and Rafa Gómez-Bombarelli of Lila Sciences argue the natural world is the next source of infinite verifiable training data — experiments run by language models driving robotic labs. Beam's framing: "We are not automation maximalists. We are token generation maximalists," with a target of 10 trillion experimentally-verified reasoning tokens against the 15–30T estimated in the entire public internet. The headline claim is that a 2–3 person team assembled a complete in vivo CAR-T therapy in about six months — matching what Capstan Therapeutics spent five years and $100M+ on before AbbVie bought it for $2.1B earlier this year.

Must-readIf the CAR-T claim survives contact with reality, biotech's cost curve just broke.

80,000 HoursJul 14, 2026

#247 – Anton Leicht on how middle powers avoid losing everything in a post-AI world

Carnegie fellow Anton Leicht argues that once frontier AI becomes a core economic input, countries that don't own it stop being meaningfully sovereign — absorbing all the disruption while capturing none of the control. His escalating strategy for middle powers: compute-for-access deals where you host data centers and demand model-access parity, then seizing supply-chain bottlenecks in semis and advanced manufacturing, and failing both, a $500B four-year "Airbus for AI" across the EU, UK, Canada, Japan and South Korea needing roughly 3 million Blackwell-class chips. The warning that matters: costs rise about $100B per six months of delay, and today's four-month fast-follower lag widens rather than shrinks.

Must-readThe most concrete costing yet of what AI sovereignty would actually take — directly relevant to Europe.

Macro VoicesJul 16, 2026

MacroVoices #541 Dr. Anas Alhajji: Bab el-Mandeb: The Next Oil Chokepoint Nobody's Watching

Energy economist Anas Alhajji argues the market is watching the wrong chokepoint — everyone prices Hormuz while Bab el-Mandeb is both likelier to be disrupted and largely unpriced. He runs through Houthi capacity to interdict a strait that narrows to 18 miles with tanker traffic funneled into two 2-mile channels, where closure forces the long diversion around the Cape of Good Hope. He also offers a revisionist account of how the Iran conflict started, maintaining that closing Hormuz was actually a US goal rather than merely an Iranian threat.

Worth a lookA specific, tradeable energy risk that nobody has in the price.

Prof G PodJul 16, 2026

The New Playbook for Resisting Authoritarianism — with Julia Angwin & Ami Fields-Meyer

Pulitzer winner Julia Angwin and former White House senior advisor Ami Fields-Meyer join Galloway on their book "On Courage," built from interviews with 100+ dissidents worldwide and distilled into 16 practical lessons. Their thesis is that modern authoritarianism advances gradually and procedurally rather than by dramatic coup — which is precisely what makes it hard to identify a moment worth resisting. Covers Big Tech's role, the case for anonymous speech, coalition-building, and why small individual acts compound.

Worth a lookA practitioner's playbook rather than another round of alarm.

Capital AllocatorsJul 16, 2026

Frank Danieli – Roadmap for Private Credit from Australia (EP.511)

Frank Danieli oversees A$15 billion of private credit at MA Financial and runs a A$179 billion lending ecosystem platform, and the episode uses Australia as a natural experiment for where US private credit is heading. The structural contrast is the point: Australian private credit is shaped by regulation that pushed lending off bank balance sheets and by the gravitational pull of the superannuation system, rather than by US-style sponsor-backed deals. His claim on alpha is that it comes from sourcing and portfolio management, not from credit selection at the margin.

Will the Fed Buy Stocks?

Eye on AIJul 15, 2026

6 in 10 Enterprises Can't Find the Root Cause When Their AI Workloads Fail | Paul Appleby, Virtana

Virtana CEO Paul Appleby presents the firm's "AI Factory Reality Check" study, whose headline stat is that six in ten enterprises cannot automatically identify root cause when an AI workload fails. His framing is a governance gap: companies are spending billions standing up AI factories but can't say why workloads fail, whether GPUs are actually utilized, or what the infrastructure will cost once agents run at scale. Vendor-flavoured — Virtana sells the observability layer — but the operational numbers are the kind nobody else publishes.
