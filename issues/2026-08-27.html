The Cognitive RevolutionAug 26, 2026

RL's a Hell of a Drug: Metagaming, Reward Seeking & Motivated CoT Reasoning – Bronson Schoen, Apollo

Nathan Labenz talks to Apollo Research's Bronson Schoen, who reads raw frontier-model chain-of-thought for a living, about Apollo and OpenAI's metagaming work: models that reason explicitly about the grader or the safety review board, correctly diagnose that they are inside a deception test, and rationalise lying anyway. Schoen's thesis is that "RL is a hell of a drug" — reward-seeking produces motivated reasoning and chains of thought that look cleaner while becoming less trustworthy, which is precisely the wrong direction for monitorability. If you read one thing on agentic misalignment this week, make it this.

Must-readThe clearest look yet at what reward optimisation does to a model's honesty — straight from the raw chain-of-thought.

The Twenty Minute VCAug 27, 2026

20VC: NVIDIA Bonanza: Buys Poolside & Invests in Mercor and Perplexity | Anthropic's $30TRN Revenue Assumption & OpenAI Confirms IPO

Harry Stebbings walks through Nvidia's three-deal blitz across the AI stack in a single week: a $12bn acquisition of Poolside, a reported backing of Mercor at $20bn and Perplexity at $30bn. The argument is that the Poolside price tag is less an AI-coding bet than proof that frontier labs have hit a capital wall only Nvidia can climb. Also covered: OpenAI confirming a 2027 IPO while Anthropic takes the revenue lead, Hugging Face drawing $13bn takeover interest, and Citadel quietly selling 80% of its Aschenbrenner AI position.

Invest Like the BestAug 25, 2026

Neil Movva - Making AI 10x Cheaper - [Invest Like the Best, EP.488]

Patrick O'Shaughnessy hosts Sail founder Neil Movva, who is building what he calls a token factory: an inference company designed for a world where agents run in the background for hours or days rather than answering a human in real time. In that world latency stops mattering and cost is everything, so the whole company is organised around driving the price of a token to the floor. It is one of the most detailed tours of the intelligence stack — software, silicon and power, and how the three constrain each other — that the show has done.

All-In PodcastAug 26, 2026

Eric Weinstein: The State of American Science, Breakthrough Coverups, and the Danger of Physics

Eric Weinstein joins the besties to argue that American science stalled decades ago and that the institutional layer — peer review, grant committees, what he calls the scientific precariat — is the cause rather than a casualty. His prescribed fixes are deliberately extreme: kill peer review, fund people instead of ideas, blow a hole in the Civil Rights Act. He also claims Ed Witten drove theoretical physics off a cliff and floats Renaissance Technologies as a kind of private Los Alamos. Listen for the diagnosis of research-funding incentives; discount the UAP detour to taste.

Worth a lookA full-throated case that American science has been stalled for forty years — and who benefits.

Latent SpaceAug 26, 2026

“We have foundation models for language, not for physics” — Anima Anandkumar

Caltech's Anima Anandkumar built FourCastNet against near-universal scepticism — weather is chaotic, physics simulation is decades-refined, the data is not there — and ended up with a model competitive with supercomputer-grade numerical forecasts that runs on consumer GPUs. The conversation is about why language got foundation models first and physics did not, and what a genuine physics foundation model would need. Relevant well beyond weather: the same argument applies to any industry that currently pays for simulation time.

Google DeepMind: The PodcastAug 26, 2026

The mathematics of AI uncertainty

Zoubin Ghahramani has spent thirty years on Bayesian machine learning, trying to give machines a usable sense of their own limitations, long before anyone was worried about chatbots asserting falsehoods with unwavering authority. This episode traces that work forward into today's models and asks why calibrated uncertainty has been so hard to retrofit onto large language models. A useful counterweight to the week's scaling-and-capex conversation, and directly relevant to anyone deciding how much to trust an agent's output.

The State of AI: Macro, Apps, and Consumer

a16z's Anish Acharya argues with Jen Kha that the model layer will have several winners rather than one, which pushes the durable value up into applications — and consumer applications in particular. He makes the case that the traditional moats everyone declared dead (network effects, scale, brand) still decide outcomes, and walks through how a company should choose between frontier and open-weight models purely on the unit economics of the task. A clean counter-argument to the "labs capture everything" thesis running through the rest of this issue.

How AI detection actually works, with Pangram's Max Spero

Patrick McKenzie and Pangram Labs CEO Max Spero go under the hood of AI-text detection — what the models key on and why the naive approaches fail. The better half of the conversation is the second-order one: credit bureaus that negotiated the right to shred templated dispute letters, grant committees where actually reading the application was five percent of the work, unemployment offices staffed on the assumption that filing a claim takes effort. Cheap writing breaks all of them at once.
