Why the Frontier Ecosystem must be Open — Matei Zaharia and Reynold Xin, Databricks

Recorded at the 2026 Data + AI Summit, Databricks co-founders Matei Zaharia (CTO) and Reynold Xin (Chief Architect) argue the frontier AI ecosystem must stay open — and that databases matter more than ever once agents start doing real work. They unpack Omnigent, Databricks' open-source meta-harness for combining and governing agents across Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and internal tools, making the case that coding agents and enterprise agents hit the same walls: portability, session history, security, and spend controls. The throughline: the industry needs a common API layer sitting above every agent harness.

The Robots Are Here

Jordan Schneider and Lily Ottinger host SemiAnalysis's Niko Ciminelli and robotics lead Reyk Knuhtsen to explain why the West keeps underestimating Unitree — which is running the DJI and BYD playbook of vertical integration and cheap actuators to make Chinese humanoids far cheaper than American ones. The core argument: robots are a genuinely general-purpose technology because, for the first time, they decouple capital from human labor. A sharp read on where the humanoid supply chain is being decided.

Eye on AIJul 02, 2026

AI Agents Are Failing and It's Almost Never the Model's Fault | Alberto Pan, Denodo

Denodo CTO Alberto Pan walks Craig Smith through the company's AI Trust Gap Report — a survey of 850 enterprise data leaders — to argue that enterprise agents rarely fail because of the model. The dominant failure modes are stale data, missing context, and inconsistent semantics across the hundreds of sources an agent must touch to do real work. After two years of pilots, his diagnosis keeps coming back to one thing: the data layer, not the LLM.
