Redwood Research (narrated blog)Jul 31, 2026

SOTA alignment assessments don't strongly update us against misalignment

Alexa Pan takes apart the alignment assessments frontier labs now publish — using Anthropic's Mythos Preview audit as the worked example — and argues they carry far weaker evidence than their conclusions imply. The structural problem is correlated failure: the audits lean on showing a model lacks the covert capability to evade detection, but a coherently misaligned model would hold exactly those capabilities, so weak training oversight and a defeated audit arrive together. She names three gaps — evaluation-awareness, under-elicitation from prompting rather than fine-tuning, and unfalsifiable sandbagging — and notes Anthropic's own auditing game failed to catch a planted misaligned model organism. Note: this is Redwood's narrated blog feed, not an interview.

Must-readThe sharpest case yet that lab safety audits prove less than their conclusions claim.

The AI Daily BriefJul 31, 2026

What a $30B Hedge Fund Implosion Really Means for AI

NLW walks through the collapse of Leopold Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness fund — roughly $45B at peak down to about $10B — which ran leverage reported near 400% on a concentrated long AI-infrastructure, short AI-disrupted-software book. Both legs broke at once: infra names off 50–78% from their peaks while the software shorts rallied, forcing margin calls and a fire-sale of the whole public book to Citadel at a discount. His thesis is that this was a leverage and market-structure failure rather than an AI-fundamentals one — lab revenues are still climbing and hyperscalers still say demand exceeds capacity — and that blowups accompany inflections without causing them.

Must-readSeparates the blowup everyone is trading off from the AI fundamentals underneath it.

Spursmál (mbl.is)Jul 31, 2026

#135 – Hverju búast menn við í aðildarsamningi?

Stefán Einar Stefánsson puts Jónas Hagan, one of the founders of the pro-EU "Já" movement, opposite Heiðrún Lind Marteinsdóttir of SFS ahead of the 29 August referendum on reopening accession negotiations. Hagan sets out what the Yes camp actually expects to obtain in an accession agreement; Heiðrún Lind argues the fisheries chapter is where Brussels sets the real price, and that no realistic deal leaves Icelandic control of the surrounding waters intact. A secondary thread on whether Icelandic coverage of the referendum is balanced. This is the third consecutive Spursmál built on the referendum, and fisheries is now the live fault line.

GZERO WorldAug 01, 2026

The UK a decade after Brexit, with Rory Stewart

Ten years on from the referendum, Rory Stewart's verdict to Ian Bremmer is that Britain bought sovereignty on paper just as the world moved to tariffs and bloc-based competition — the exact environment in which going it alone costs most. His clearest symptom is seven prime ministers in ten years. They turn to Nigel Farage and Reform UK, where recent financial controversies cut against the anti-establishment positioning, and to Manchester mayor Andy Burnham as the emerging alternative pole in British politics.

War on the RocksJul 31, 2026

The Atlantic Brief: The Strait of Hormuz and the Future of Free Navigation

The first joint RUSI / War on the Rocks panel, framed around the Iran war opened in February and the oscillation since between strikes, a fragile ceasefire and closure of the Strait of Hormuz, with open hostilities resuming in mid-July. The panel — Vice Admiral (ret.) Andrew Burns, Nicholas Hopton, Rachel Ziemba and Jonathan Eyal — treats Hormuz not as an isolated chokepoint crisis but as the test case for a wider erosion of free navigation, extending the analysis to the Houthi threat through Bab al-Mandab. Burns brings the naval operational read, Ziemba the energy and shipping-cost consequences.

The Prof G PodAug 01, 2026

No Mercy / No Malice: Shoulders of Giants

Galloway goes after the self-made myth with numbers: Chuck Collins finds that although roughly 70% of the Forbes 400 are labelled self-made, only about a third actually came from disadvantaged backgrounds. He names the tech figures who took public money while campaigning against government — some $2.5bn in government support behind Tesla, browser technology out of publicly funded institutions — and cites Berkeley work in which subjects primed to feel upper-class took roughly twice as much candy. The prescription is a Marshall Plan raising four-year college enrolment 40% and trade schools 80%, funded by taxing $1bn+ endowments that refuse to expand admissions.

Worth a lookGalloway's strongest format, and the enrolment numbers carry the whole argument.

a16z PodcastJul 31, 2026

How Enterprise AI Really Gets Deployed

Decagon moved most of its inference off frontier APIs onto open-source models, and founders Jesse Zhang and Ashwin Sreenivas give Sarah Wang and Kimberly Tan the operator-level account of why: latency, evaluation and fine-tuning — not raw model quality — are the binding constraints on production agents. The thesis is that enterprise AI is mostly forward-deployed engineering plus enterprise sales, which is the case for application companies thriving alongside the labs rather than being absorbed by them. Eighty minutes, and unusually concrete about cost structure.

Worth a lookThe real cost of shipping agents, from a company that moved off the frontier APIs.
