Are Rumors of an Imminent Russian Attack Warranted? | Yaroslav Trofimov

Yaroslav Trofimov, the Wall Street Journal's chief foreign affairs correspondent, argues the risk has shifted from Ukraine to NATO itself — that Moscow may escalate specifically to test whether Article 5 means anything. He connects CIA director John Ratcliffe's recent Moscow visit to the wider picture: Western missile stockpiles drained by US entanglement in the Middle East, a corruption shake-up inside Zelensky's cabinet, and China quietly preferring a war neither side wins. Released this morning, so it is the freshest read on European escalation risk you will find today.

Prof G MarketsSep 04, 2026

Tyler Cowen: The AI Bears Are Asking The Wrong Questions

Tyler Cowen tells Scott Galloway and Ed Elson there is no AI bubble worth fearing and that the data-centre buildout is net positive — the bear case, he argues, is aimed at the wrong variable entirely. The substance is in where he thinks the gains actually get captured, how AI reshapes the labour market, and the single constraint he names as the real ceiling on further AI growth. His rising risk is not valuations but cybersecurity.

Fei-Fei Li: The Race to Build World Models For AI

Fei-Fei Li, Justin Johnson and Ben Mildenhall of World Labs walk Martin Casado through Atlas, their world model, and the "new view prediction" approach that fuses generative modelling with 3D reconstruction. What Could Shake Up Markets in September?

Morgan Stanley's Andrew Sheets, Global Head of Fixed Income Research, puts a number on it: markets are pricing roughly a two-in-three chance of a rate hike at the 16 September Fed meeting, with inflation still above target.
