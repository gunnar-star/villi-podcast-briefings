Complex SystemsJul 30, 2026

Cheap cognition, supercharged surveillance, and AI risks, with Garrison Lovely

Patrick McKenzie hosts Garrison Lovely, author of “Obsolete: The AI Industry’s Trillion-Dollar Race to Replace Us,” to map the three-cornered AI-risk debate — doomers, ethicists, accelerationists — and why the camps keep talking past each other. The sharper half is about what cheap cognition does to surveillance infrastructure that already exists: FinCEN takes ~4 million suspicious-activity reports a year and reads almost none of them, ICE runs ~1 million queries a year against that database, and transcribing every podcast ever recorded now costs approximately nothing. The point isn’t new capability — it’s that dormant data becomes searchable the moment reading gets free.

Must-readThe AI-risk conversation that’s actually about deployed systems, not thought experiments.

MoonshotsJul 29, 2026

Dario vs Jensen on Open Weights, OpenAI & Anthropic in DC, Xi Exports AI to Global South | EP #275

Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross work through the open-vs-closed fight that has Dario Amodei and Jensen Huang on opposite sides — Nvidia wants weights everywhere because it sells the compute, Anthropic argues proliferation is the risk. They also cover OpenAI and Anthropic jointly lobbying in Washington (former rivals, now a bloc) and Kimi K3’s international push. Useful as the fastest read on why the open-weights argument is now a policy fight rather than a technical one.

All-In PodcastJul 28, 2026

The $1/Hour Worker: Four Robotics CEOs on Humanoids at Home, China’s Threat, and the End of Dangerous Jobs

Four robotics CEOs in one episode: ANYbotics’ Péter Fankhauser on why he bet the company on four-legged robots rather than humanoids (and calls China’s armed robot dogs “stupid and risky”), NEO’s Bernt Børnich claiming robots-building-robots within three years and a hard takeoff, and Boston Dynamics’ Amanda McMaster on Spot being the most-deployed robot on earth. The through-line is unit economics — what happens to labour markets when embodied work prices at a dollar an hour.

Eye on AIJul 29, 2026

“According to NASA’s Definition of Life, I’m Not Alive” — Why Nobody Can Define Life | Dr. Kate Adamala

Kate Adamala’s lab at Minnesota just published a landmark paper on “spud cells” — the first cell assembled entirely from purified off-the-shelf molecules that can feed itself, grow, and divide through its own genetic activity. She walks Craig Smith through what they actually built and what stands between a proof of concept and a platform that could replace petrochemical feedstocks. Synthetic biology’s equivalent of a working prototype, from the person who built it.

Must-readA genuine first in synthetic biology, explained by the researcher who did it.

Doom DebatesJul 28, 2026

A Google DeepMind Policy Lead Mocks the AI Pause — and Why That Matters

Liron Shapira dissects a public exchange in which Sébastien Krier, Frontier Policy Development Lead at Google DeepMind, replied to a call for coordinated global capability slowdown with a dismissive meme. His two objections: nobody now knows whether Demis Hassabis’s stated openness to coordination is actually DeepMind’s position, and the framing caricatures what pause advocates propose. Short, pointed, and a useful read on how frontier-lab policy staff actually treat slowdown proposals when they think no one’s grading.
