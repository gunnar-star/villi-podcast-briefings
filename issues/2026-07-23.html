Latent SpaceJul 23, 2026

Inside the Model Factory — Eiso Kant, Poolside AI

Poolside CEO Eiso Kant details the "Model Factory": an industrialized pipeline that takes a model from pre-training to release in roughly eight weeks, and claims its new Laguna S 2.1 beats Thinking Machines' recent release at about a tenth of the parameter count. He recounts spending $12M building code language models before anyone cared, now vindicated as the field races toward efficient, ownable models. Frames the escalating open-vs-closed and U.S.-vs-China fight over who controls frontier and sovereign AI.

Kimi K3, Xi's Speech, and China's Mythos Moment

The ChinaTalk team unpacks Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 — a ~2.8-trillion-parameter open-source model released July 17 — alongside Xi Jinping's WAIC speech in Shanghai framing open source as "a public good for humanity," with model-approval times cut from ~60 days to about a week. They lay out three scenarios for where Chinese AI governance heads next. The throughline: China is turning open-weight releases into geopolitical soft power aimed squarely at closed American labs.

Invest Like the BestJul 21, 2026

Matthew Smith — The Coming U.S. Natural Gas Shortage

Chronometer Partners founder/CIO Matthew Smith spent ~18 months modeling nearly every U.S. natural gas well, pipeline, and processing asset, and argues LNG exports plus AI/compute electricity demand are creating a structural gas shortage. He warns of an unprecedented shortage beginning around 2028, with working storage potentially exhausted by 2030, because infrastructure bottlenecks cap new supply and large-scale nuclear is the only durable fix. A concrete, contrarian macro-energy thesis with direct investing implications.

Latent SpaceJul 21, 2026

Causal Models Need Causal Data — Xaira's X-Cell Model for Drug Discovery

Xaira's Bo Wang and Ci Chu explain a scaling-law problem in biology: test loss flatlined past ~1.5B parameters even as training loss kept falling, proving their model was bottlenecked by information in the data, not compute or parameter count. The fix was building roughly 30x more information-rich data for predicting gene-expression changes, restoring the ability to scale performance. It underpins their X-Cell foundation model for drug discovery and is a sharp lesson in when more compute stops helping.

Worth a lookA clean demonstration that scaling laws in biology break on data quality, not compute — with a fix.

Eye on AIJul 21, 2026

"According to NASA's Definition of Life, I'm Not Alive" — Dr. Kate Adamala

Synthetic biologist Kate Adamala describes her lab's landmark "spud cells" — the first cell assembled entirely from purified, off-the-shelf molecules that can feed itself, grow, and split into daughter cells through its own genetic activity. She explains what her team actually built and what it takes to move from proof of concept toward broader use. The provocative hook: under NASA's formal definition of life, engineered constructs like these don't qualify — a reminder no one can cleanly define life.
