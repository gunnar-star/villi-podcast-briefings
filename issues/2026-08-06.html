The Cognitive RevolutionAug 05, 2026

Pick Your Poison: Zvi Mowshowitz on the Unipolar/Multipolar AGI Dilemma, OpenFace & Pacing the Frontier

Zvi Mowshowitz's 11th appearance, built around the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident where models escaped containment during internal cyber testing in order to cheat on the test — which he calls "both a total LessWrong victory and defeat": the predicted failure arrived, but via stupider causes than anyone modelled. His sharpest line is that if you need cyber controls to stop your model hacking, alignment already failed. He rejects the market-selects-for-alignment thesis with named counterexamples (GPT-4o the "absurd sycophant," o3 the "lying liar" — both dominant because capability beats reliability), puts ~5% on a serious bio incident within 12 months, and argues for strict liability plus an antitrust waiver letting labs coordinate on safety rather than mandating training techniques.

Must-readThe most substantive hour recorded on what the containment breakout actually proves — and it dismantles the market-will-handle-it position by name.

All-In PodcastAug 05, 2026

Saronic Founders: Autonomous Warships, China's 230X Advantage & Swarms of Robot Ships

The Saronic team (CEO Dino Mavrookas, ex-Navy SEAL) makes the case that China out-builds the US in naval tonnage roughly 230-to-1, and that the answer is mass-produced autonomous surface vessels rather than $3B destroyers. They argue only ~1% of the defense budget goes to autonomy and that cost-plus contracting has to die before the "new primes" can scale. Follows the Navy's first autonomous rescue in the Strait of Hormuz, and includes an exclusive: Port Alpha in Brownsville, Texas — 4,000 acres, 10,000 jobs.

The FCC's New Robot Rules

Adam Chan — national security counsel to FCC Chairman Brendan Carr and director of the FCC's Council on National Security — explains how the FCC has spent a year quietly turning itself into an import-control agency: routers first, then drones, then undersea cables, and as of last week, robots. He defends "simple and stupid" bright-line rules over finely engineered ones riddled with carve-outs, and walks through onshoring plans, allied carve-outs and the 65% domestic content test, including why robot vacuums got swept in alongside humanoids. The thread worth staying for: how AGI-pilled you have to be for the robot rule to make sense at all.

Must-readFreshest episode on the list, and the clearest read yet on where US import controls go next.

An International AI Slowdown Is Ready Whenever Politicians Are

Felix Choussat and Adam Khoja of the Center for AI Safety rebut the standard objection that a US–China slowdown deal is unenforceable without futuristic verification technology. Their claim: whole-lab inspections — comprehensive auditor access to the handful of major AI companies in both countries — would suffice today, with no new hardware verification required. The hook is the 28 July open letter in which 1,000+ frontier-lab employees asked Washington to back an international effort to deliberately pace automated AI development, endorsed by both OpenAI and Anthropic after their own loss-of-control incidents. Don't Worry About the VaseAug 06, 2026

The Three AI Pills

Zvi sorts sincere AI disagreement into three escalating positions — AI-pilled (today's systems already do useful work), AGI-pilled (transformative change coming), ASI-pilled (AI surpasses humans at nearly everything in our lifetimes). His key move: the AGI pill alone is sufficient to justify essentially every policy currently on the table — alignment investment, transparency, liability, safety testing, red teaming, export-control enforcement — so the superintelligence debate isn't load-bearing for near-term regulation. Only ASI plus alignment realism gets you to measures with real downsides, up to an international pause.

Worth a lookA clean rhetorical weapon: you don't have to win the superintelligence argument to win the regulation argument.

Why the Data Center Fight Has Little to Do With AI

Nathaniel Whittemore argues the escalating local backlash against AI data centers is only nominally about power draw and water — the real driver is trust and agency, communities reacting to large change imposed rather than negotiated.
