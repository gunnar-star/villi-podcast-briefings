Odd LotsJul 20, 2026

The Creator of Claude Code on The Hottest Piece of Software in the World

Boris Cherny, the Anthropic engineer who built Claude Code, walks Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway through how an agentic coding tool went from an internal experiment to what they call the hottest piece of software in the world. His core argument: coding agents don't just make developers faster, they threaten the business model of legacy software vendors, because the marginal cost of building bespoke software is collapsing. A concrete, first-hand look at why "the AI writes the code now" has finally become more than a slogan.

Prof G MarketsJul 20, 2026

OpenAI Is Spinning Out Of Sam Altman's Control

Scott Galloway makes a concrete, falsifiable call: OpenAI replaces Sam Altman as CEO within six months, acquires enterprise-AI firm Sierra, and installs co-founder Bret Taylor as "the adult in the room" — his read being that Altman is an innovator, not an operator. Galloway and Ed Elson also argue China's fast-following models are a genuine competitive threat and push listeners to diversify into low-AI-exposure sectors rather than crowd further into Big Tech.

Prof G MarketsJul 17, 2026

Mike Novogratz: Crypto's Investors Have Moved On

Galaxy Digital's Mike Novogratz argues crypto's "age of speculation" is ending — the young traders who once chased Solana are now piling into memory-chip names like SK Hynix, sports betting and same-day options. He explains his own pivot to becoming an "AI landlord" in data-center infrastructure, and warns the AI boom itself may be heading toward a demand crisis rather than an endless build-out.

Capital AllocatorsJul 20, 2026

Senior Decision Makers: Luis Laboy, Hewlett Foundation — Ep. 512

Luis Laboy, who has run public equities at the William & Flora Hewlett Foundation for roughly a decade alongside CIO Ana Marshall, unpacks how one of the largest US private foundations actually makes portfolio decisions. The conversation digs into public-equity construction, emerging-markets exposure, and the softer question of how senior investors build judgment and delegate inside a large endowment. A useful window into long-horizon institutional allocation for anyone thinking about how their own book is run.
