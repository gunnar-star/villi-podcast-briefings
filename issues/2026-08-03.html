AXRPAug 2, 2026

50 – Eli Lifland on AI 2027

Eli Lifland, co-author of the AI 2027 scenario, walks Daniel Filan through how the forecast was actually constructed — time-horizon growth curves, effective-compute projections, and the jump from superhuman coder to superintelligence. The back half is the uncomfortable part: what a misaligned AGI would want, whether AIs can align their successors, and why the scenario ships with two endings. Two and a half hours, and the clearest account anywhere of the reasoning behind the most-cited AGI timeline.

The Cognitive RevolutionAug 2, 2026

Nathan Goes to China – Part 2: AI Safety with Chinese Characteristics

Nathan Labenz reports back from two weeks in China — WAIC in Shanghai and the launch of an AI safety hub at Tsinghua — to test the American policy claim that safety obligations are futile because China won't reciprocate. He finds Chinese models do carry weaker safeguards, but argues the gap shrinks sharply once you stop comparing all of China to just OpenAI and Anthropic, and traces the "45-degree line" doctrine that capability and safety should rise together.

Must-readField evidence against the single most load-bearing argument in US AI policy.

Hidden ForcesAug 3, 2026

The AI Enshittification Bubble | Cory Doctorow

Cory Doctorow argues the AI unwind will do more damage than 2008, because the companies leading the boom are structurally unprofitable and existentially dependent on a growth narrative that has to keep accelerating. Demetri Kofinas pushes him on where value actually accrues once it deflates. Set against the forced liquidations of the past two weeks, it reads less like a rant than a thesis.

LessWrong (Curated & Popular)Aug 2, 2026

Thousand-dimensional structure — Geoffrey Irving & David Africa

Irving (UK AI Security Institute) makes the case that alignment is only tractable if model behaviour has low-dimensional structure: nobody can hand-specify a trillion parameters, but you might steer a handful of directions that control most behaviour. He builds on the emergent-misalignment results where fine-tuning on insecure code made models broadly misaligned across unrelated tasks. Short, technical, and the most concrete optimistic case on offer.

Worth a lookWhy alignment might be tractable at all, stated precisely enough to be wrong.
